How to Eliminate "Per-Envelope" E-Signature Fees for Good

Pay-per-envelope pricing was designed for a world where sending a document was rare. Most teams now send constantly, and the meter has become a tax on doing the work.

SJ Sarah Jenkins 14 JUL 2026 FINANCE & OPS 5 MIN READ
DocuFence

Introduction

Every finance lead who has renewed an e-signature contract knows the conversation. Usage was higher than forecast, the envelope allowance ran out in month nine, and someone in procurement is now asking why a signature costs money at all.

The awkward part is that the overage is rarely caused by growth in revenue. It is caused by ordinary operational hygiene: a re-send after a typo, a second signer added late, a countersignature that needed its own envelope.

The Hidden Cost

The line item on the invoice is the smallest part of the cost. The expensive part is the behaviour the meter creates.

Batching. Teams hold documents back to avoid burning envelopes, which pushes execution dates later in the month.
Shadow signing. Someone prints, signs, scans, and emails a PDF to avoid the cost, and the audit trail disappears with it.
Seat rationing. Only two people hold licences, so every signature routes through them and stalls when they are away.
A pricing model that makes your team hesitate before sending a document is not a pricing model. It is a process constraint.

What Teams Actually Pay

A 40-person company sending offer letters, vendor agreements, and renewals typically lands between 900 and 1,400 envelopes a year once re-sends are counted. On a metered plan with overage, that is where the surprise lives.

Nearly a quarter of all sends are corrections. On a metered plan you pay twice for the same agreement, and the second charge buys you nothing.

The Solution

Move signing into the tool where the document already lives, on a plan where sending is unlimited. Three things change immediately.

01
No second copy. The signature lands on the file you were already editing, so there is no re-upload and no version to reconcile.
02
Correcting is free. A typo costs a minute, not a billable envelope, so nobody negotiates with the meter before fixing an error.
03
Everyone can send. Signing stops being a licensed privilege, which removes the bottleneck around two people's calendars.

Migrating in a Week

Start with one document family rather than the whole estate. Offer letters and vendor NDAs are ideal: high volume, low variation, and owned by a single team.

Export completed envelopes from the incumbent for the record, point the template at the new workspace, and run both in parallel for a fortnight. Executed agreements stay valid either way, so there is no cliff edge.

What to Watch For

Unlimited sending is only worth having if the record is intact. Before you switch, confirm three things in writing: that every signature carries a time stamp and an audit trail, that access can be revoked after a document has been opened, and that your files are never used to train a model.

If a vendor cannot answer those three questions plainly, the envelope price is not the thing that should worry you.
